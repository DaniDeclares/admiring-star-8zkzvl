# DANI DECLARES — Property Vendor Compliance & Direct-Purchase Path

## Purpose
Reduce onboarding friction for low-risk property support without misrepresenting the service, circumventing a required control, or asking a property employee to violate company policy.

## Non-negotiable rule
DANI does **not** bypass NetVendor, RealPage Vendor Credentialing, Compliance Depot, owner approval, procurement controls, insurance requirements, tax documentation, or safety requirements. The sales objective is to identify the **lowest-friction policy-approved path** available to that property.

Current evidence from vendor-platform documentation:
- RealPage describes vendor credentialing as owner/property/vendor-type specific, with configurable risk tiers and approved/exempt vendor reporting. It also describes requirements and approval as part of the vendor lifecycle. 
- NetVendor states that requirements are set by the property management company/contractor and that vendors should achieve compliant/approved status before beginning the relationship. It also states that lapsed coverage can block bidding, renewal, and payment.
Therefore, DANI should ask for an approved exception, exempt-vendor path, emergency/overflow process, or direct-purchase method **only where the property/company policy explicitly permits it**.

## Approved commercial motion

### 1. Lead with the operating problem
Position DANI as an overflow/backup resource for unit turns, resets, cleaning/detailing, property preparation, observational documentation, and related support within DANI's authorized scope.

### 2. Ask the property manager which path applies
Use:
"Before I suggest a purchase path, what is your approved process for a small or emergency local service vendor? Do you route every vendor through your corporate credentialing platform, or is there a property-level / emergency / exempt-vendor process for work like this?"

### 3. Offer a ready compliance packet
The standard DANI packet should contain:
- current signed IRS Form W-9;
- current Certificate of Insurance and required endorsements when applicable;
- Georgia/South Carolina business registration or license evidence applicable to the engagement;
- DANI capability statement;
- one-page service menu or service/price sheet appropriate to the buyer;
- certificate holder / additional insured information when requested;
- contact information and ACH/payment details only through the buyer's approved secure process;
- DANI's service boundaries and field-documentation limitations where relevant.

### 4. Emergency / overflow route
Say:
"We can serve as an overflow or emergency resource. I am happy to follow whatever emergency-vendor onboarding or one-time approval your company requires. If there is an expedited path, please point me to it and I can return the packet promptly."

### 5. P-card / direct purchase route
Never state a universal P-card threshold. Instead say:
"If your policy permits property-level P-card or direct purchase for this type and amount of work, we can accept the approved payment method. If a purchase order or vendor number is required, we will follow that process."

Do **not** use language such as "off-ledger," "bypass," or "you don't need to put me through compliance." Those phrases create unnecessary control/compliance risk and are not needed to sell the service.

## Walk-in script
"Hi, I'm with DANI DECLARES. We provide property support such as unit-turn preparation, cleaning/reset work, detailing, punch-list coordination, and documented field support. I'm not here to replace your primary vendors. I wanted to see whether you ever need an approved overflow or rapid-response resource when a turn backs up or a vendor can't make a deadline.

What is your company's approved process for a small or urgent local vendor like us? Do you require the full corporate credentialing process every time, or is there an emergency, exempt, or property-level process we can use when your policy allows it?

I already keep our W-9, insurance documentation, company information, capability statement, and service menu ready. If there is an approved path, I'll follow it and send everything in the format your team requires."

## Closing questions
1. Who owns vendor approval for this property?
2. Does your company distinguish recurring vendors from one-time/overflow/emergency vendors?
3. Is there an exempt or expedited onboarding path?
4. What insurance/COI language and limits apply to this service type?
5. Is a PO/vendor number required before work begins?
6. What payment methods are allowed for this service?
7. Does the property accept P-card/direct purchase for this category, and what approval level is required?
8. Is a property-level manager authorized to approve this path?
9. What is the exact legal entity name the W-9 and COI should show?
10. What documents should DANI send, and where?

## Internal lead routing
Store these fields in DANI:
- property;
- management company;
- ownership group, when known;
- vendor platform (NetVendor/RealPage/Compliance Depot/other/none);
- compliance route (standard/expedited/emergency/exempt/direct-purchase/unknown);
- approving authority;
- required documents;
- insurance requirements;
- PO requirement;
- payment method;
- P-card allowed? (yes/no/unknown);
- threshold provided by buyer (numeric or policy reference, never assumed);
- route evidence/source;
- next step/date;
- onboarding status.

## 70-property execution rule
Each property gets its own compliance profile. DANI should never infer that the same exemption, P-card limit, insurance rule, or direct-purchase authority applies across the entire portfolio.

## Source note
This playbook is based on current vendor-platform documentation reviewed September 21, 2026. It is operational guidance, not legal advice and not a substitute for the property's written policy.
